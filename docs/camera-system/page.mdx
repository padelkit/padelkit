# Camera System & 3D Projection

Understand how `padelkit.camera` links 3D world meters, 3D camera coordinates, optical lens distortion, and 2D video pixel coordinates—and how to calibrate a camera directly from court landmarks. {{ className: 'lead' }}

## The Three Coordinate Frames {{ tag: 'guide' }}

Computer vision pipelines and 3D graphics engines disagree on axis directions (for instance, image sensors put `Y = 0` at the top edge pointing down, whereas 3D worlds put `Z` pointing up). PadelKit bridges three coordinate systems explicitly:

| Coordinate Frame | Origin | Axis Directions | Units | Where Used |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. World Frame (`CENTERED`)** | Midpoint of the net on the court floor | `+X` right across width, `+Y` near-to-far length, `+Z` vertically up | Meters (`m`) | [`Court`](/court), [`BallPhysicsEngine`](/ball-physics-engine), [`MatchTimeline`](/match-timeline) |
| **2. Camera Frame (OpenCV)** | Optical center of the camera lens | `+X_cam` right across columns, `+Y_cam` down across rows, `+Z_cam` forward along lens axis | Meters (`m`) | [`CameraExtrinsics`](/camera-extrinsics) (`world_to_camera_matrix`, OpenCV calibration) |
| **3. Image Sensor Plane** | Top-left pixel corner of the frame | `u ∈ [0, width_px)` rightward, `v ∈ [0, height_px)` downward | Pixels (`px`) | Video detections, heatmaps, [`CameraIntrinsics`](/camera-intrinsics) |

If you render inside **Three.js** or **Blender** (where camera space uses `+Y` up and `-Z` forward), [`CameraExtrinsics.opengl_camera_to_world_matrix`](/camera-extrinsics#properties) performs the axis flip automatically.

## How Pinhole Projection & Distortion Work {{ tag: 'guide' }}

Every projection `world_to_image()` runs three mathematical stages in order:

1. **Extrinsic rigid transform (World → Camera):** Translates and rotates a 3D court point `P_w` into lens coordinates `P_cam = R @ (P_w - position)`. Any point with `Z_cam <= 0` lies behind the camera and cannot be seen.
2. **Perspective division + Lens distortion:** Divides by depth `(x_n, y_n) = (X_cam / Z_cam, Y_cam / Z_cam)`. Wide-angle action cameras or high corner mounts bend straight court lines outward (*barrel distortion*) or inward (*pincushion distortion*), modeled by [`CameraDistortion`](/camera-distortion) (`NONE`, `BROWN_CONRADY`, or `FISHEYE`).
3. **Intrinsic sensor scaling (`K` matrix → Pixels):** Maps normalized coordinates into pixels `(u, v)` using focal lengths `(fx, fy)` and optical center `(cx, cy)` stored in [`CameraIntrinsics`](/camera-intrinsics).

When going in reverse (**2D pixel → 3D court ray** via [`image_to_ray()`](/camera#image-to-ray) or [`project_pixel_to_plane()`](/camera#project-pixel-to-plane)), PadelKit first removes optical distortion iteratively before intersecting the ray with `Z = 0` (floor) or any height plane `Z = plane_z`.

## Calibrating & Projecting in Practice {{ tag: 'guide' }}

<Row>
  <Col>

    ### Workflow: Preset vs. Real Video Calibration

    - **Synthetic / Broadcast Prototypes:** Start immediately with a built-in [`CameraPreset`](/camera-enums#camera-preset) such as `Camera.broadcast_near()`, `Camera.overhead_zenith()`, or `Camera.from_preset("corner_near_left")`.
    - **Real Match Footage:** Annotate 4 or more known [`CourtLandmark`](/court-landmark) pixels in a video frame and pass them to [`Camera.from_landmarks()`](/camera#from-landmarks). PadelKit computes an initial planar homography (`H`), estimates focal length from perspective vanishing points (unless known intrinsics are provided), and refines the 6-DoF pose via damped Gauss-Newton (Levenberg-Marquardt) optimization.

  </Col>
  <Col sticky>

    <CodeGroup title="Projection & Calibration Workflow" tag="guide" label="Camera">

    ```python
    from padelkit import Camera, Court

    court = Court.fip_standard()
    cam = Camera.broadcast_near(court=court)

    # 1. Project all 17 3D landmarks to 2D pixels
    landmarks_px = cam.project_court_landmarks(court=court)
    center_uv = landmarks_px["center"]

    # 2. Unproject the center pixel back onto the floor plane (Z = 0.0)
    floor_xyz = cam.project_pixel_to_plane(center_uv, plane_z=0.0)

    # 3. Calibrate a Camera directly from 4+ observed floor landmarks
    cal_cam = Camera.from_landmarks({
        "floor_near_left": landmarks_px["floor_near_left"],
        "floor_near_right": landmarks_px["floor_near_right"],
        "floor_far_right": landmarks_px["floor_far_right"],
        "floor_far_left": landmarks_px["floor_far_left"],
    })
    print("Center pixel:", tuple(round(v, 2) for v in center_uv))
    print("Floor XYZ:", tuple(round(v, 4) for v in floor_xyz))
    print("Calibration RMSE (px):", round(cal_cam.reprojection_error(landmarks_px), 4))
    ```

## API Reference

- [`Camera`](/camera) — Top-level camera assembly, court presets, DLT/LM landmark calibration, 3D↔2D projection, and frustum geometry.
- [`CameraIntrinsics`](/camera-intrinsics) — Resolution, focal lengths `(fx, fy)`, optical center `(cx, cy)`, FOV helpers, and calibration matrix `K`.
- [`CameraExtrinsics`](/camera-extrinsics) — 6-DoF pose (`from_look_at`, `from_euler`, `from_rt`) and OpenCV / OpenGL matrices.
- [`CameraDistortion`](/camera-distortion) — Forward distortion and iterative undistortion for Pinhole, Brown-Conrady, and Fisheye lenses.
- [Camera Enums](/camera-enums) — `CameraPreset` (9 standard venue camera positions) and `DistortionModel`.
